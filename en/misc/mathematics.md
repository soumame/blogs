I started university ([[en/works/keio|university]]) in 2025, and my first semester is already over, but in some of the classes I’m taking in the second semester I need high school math knowledge, and I’m in a pretty bad spot.

> Calculus and similar topics are coming up at university, and I’m paying the price for not having studied them in high school... (In the Australian curriculum they’re optional, and I only did the equivalent of Mathematics I and A.) It’s almost strange that I never needed them before when writing code.
>
[Quote from X](https://x.com/So_to9/status/2068907852870418614)

I attended [[en/works/pisa|an Australian international school in Malaysia]], and as part of the Victoria curriculum (VCE) I took a subject called "General Mathematics". In this curriculum’s mathematics, there are three types:

- General Mathematics
- Mathematical Methods
- Specialist Mathematics

As the names suggest, General Mathematics is a basic level, and Specialist is for people who like math and want to study it more deeply. I treated math as a tool and didn’t like it that much, so I took General Mathematics.

The scope covered in General Mathematics corresponds to Mathematics I and A in Japan. Its biggest features are that it assumes the use of a CAS calculator (a graphing calculator) and that it includes financial mathematics, matrices, and network theory, which aren’t part of the Japanese curriculum.

Students aiming for STEM fields are required to take Mathematical Methods; without it, you may not be able to enter some science/engineering faculties. This covers the equivalent of Mathematics II, B, and C.

Specialist Mathematics is the most advanced option, usually taken by those aiming for engineering and similar fields, and is said to be at Mathematics III level plus university preparatory level.

I love engineering and am basically a STEM person, but I took General, so I’m still struggling quite a bit...

But this system isn’t necessarily bad; I actually think it might be better than Japan’s. In Japan, even humanities students need a fairly high level, and in general academic tracks you probably have to reach around the level of Mathematical Methods. That may help raise the overall math level of the population, but if it’s all crammed in and then forgotten, I feel the effectiveness is diminished.

Also, the financial mathematics covered in General Mathematics is quite useful — I find myself recalling it when doing things like investing in mutual funds or depositing money — so I’m glad I learned it.

Personally, I also think it was pretty good to have covered network theory and matrices earlier — they end up being used quite a bit. In Japan these are often expressed with formulas, but in Australia you’ll encounter calculations for network theory and matrices without many formulas. You can even skip the calculation details and input them into the calculator. In General Mathematics, math is treated as a tool for everyday life.
