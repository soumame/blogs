Since I entered [[en/works/keio|university]] in 2025, the first semester has already ended, but in some of the classes I'm taking in the second semester I need high school math knowledge, and I'm in a pretty bad state.

> Calculus is coming up at university and the price of not having studied it in high school is catching up... (In the Australian curriculum it was optional, and I only did the equivalent of Mathematics I and A.) It's strange I never needed it even when I was coding until now.
> 
[Quote from X](https://x.com/So_to9/status/2068907852870418614)

I attended [[en/works/pisa|an Australian international school in Malaysia]], and under the Victoria curriculum (VCE) I took a subject called “General Mathematics.” In this curriculum there are three types of math:

- General Mathematics
- Mathematical Methods
- Specialist Mathematics

As the names largely indicate, General Mathematics is at a basic level, while Specialist is for people who like math and intend to study it more seriously. I treated math as a tool and didn’t like it that much, so I took General Mathematics.

The scope covered by General Mathematics corresponds roughly to Mathematics I and Mathematics A in Japan. Its biggest features are that it assumes the use of a CAS calculator (a graphing calculator) and that it includes topics not found in Japanese curricula, like financial mathematics, matrices, and network theory.

People aiming for STEM fields are required to take Mathematical Methods, and if you don’t take it you might not be able to enter some science faculties. That course covers the equivalents of Mathematics II, B, and C.

Specialist Mathematics is the most advanced, often taken by those aiming for engineering; it’s said to be at the level of Mathematics III plus some university-level material.

I love engineering and am what you’d call a science student, yet I took General, so I’m still struggling a lot...

But this system isn’t necessarily bad — I actually think it might be better than Japan’s. In Japan, even humanities students need a decent level, and in general academic tracks they probably go up to about the level of Mathematical Methods. That may raise the overall math level of the population, but if it becomes just cramming and people forget it, I feel the effect is diminished.

Also, the financial math covered in General Mathematics is quite useful (I recall it when investing in mutual funds or depositing money), so I’m glad I learned it. Personally, I also think that learning network theory and matrices early might have been pretty good — you end up using them a lot. In Japan these are often expressed with formulas, but in Australia network theory and matrix calculations appear without many formulas. They even skip the calculation details and input them into the calculator. In General Mathematics, math feels treated as a tool for everyday life.
