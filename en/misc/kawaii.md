## I Want to Be Cute
At least in Japan, there are fairly strong gender roles, and I think there's a social concept of gender that's divided into two based on the biological characteristics of male and female.

For me, when I see someone's appearance or behavior and think "Oh, they're cute...", it's almost always someone who is socially regarded as female. And people who behave socially as women are generally considered biologically female as well (at least the statistics used to say that).

But I'm socially a man, and maybe once every few dozen times I have a moment where I think "I want to be like that." I've been exposed to the internet since I was little, and I find people who have the kinds of looks that are popular there to be "cute."

## I Want to Be a Girl... Not Exactly?
But it's not that I want to change my biological sex to female right this moment. I don't find being otherwise painful, and I don't identify as a woman.

### What Even Is "Cute"
可憐 (karen): an appearance or manner that is beautiful and soothing to the heart.

I think my idea of "cute" refers to that kind of delicate/charming quality.

#### What Is "Cute" For?
So why do we want to be cute? Why do we want to make ourselves look attractive?

If you think about that, it seems necessary to ask why we want to make ourselves look attractive in the first place.

First, I think the standard of beauty itself is decided by society. The appearance that is supported as "beautiful" varies by era. Humans have reproduced as a species up to now, and when choosing partners it's not uncommon to use appearance as one criterion.

I think it's interesting that society sets those standards. I don't know what other animals do, but humans wear clothes, put on makeup (in some eras people even smeared toxic substances on their faces), and deliberately remove body hair that is part of their body.

Maybe we're adjusting ourselves according to social standards to improve short-term impressions. Short-term impressions are very important for attracting people, opportunities, and resources. Also, I don't know about the long term, but it's likely strongly connected to opportunities for sexual contact, which are essential for human survival.

But I also think being someone who can communicate attractively becomes important in the long run.

Not just "cute" but also words like "cool" exist; the metrics for judging appearance are very fuzzy and perhaps multidimensional.

### Reasons I Want to Try Being Cute
I've often seen people who use those short-term impressions to give good impressions and attract various things, and since I don't feel like I've experienced that myself, I end up feeling envious. But when you recognize yourself as male and live socially as a man, you don't pay much attention to male attractiveness; rather, you tend to focus on the female side. So, rather than wanting to improve beauty along the axis of being a man, you might want to improve along the axis of being a woman — in other words, you might want to become cute.

#### Social Changes
And in this society that has continued since agrarian times, the roles of women and men have been quite different, and women have often been expected to prioritize appearance. The typical pattern was men working and women doing housework.

But recently that's been changing, and more people choose to work in the same way as men, so the big differences seem to be diminishing.

So, when comparing men and women on the same field, it feels like women have one more thing to do. Women may shift toward another axis—what they can do—beyond appearance, while men, for whom skills matter too, may also begin caring about appearance. Whether this difference will eventually be closed and social gender disappear is doubtful even from a biological viewpoint, so I think it will remain; but compared to the past, differences like "men work, women do housework" are shrinking.

I think this is especially pronounced in Japan recently; in places I used to live like Malaysia, religion has a large influence and it hasn't become like this. Oh, but in Korea male idols wear a lot of makeup, elaborate clothes, and dance a lot. Maybe that's the kind of thing I'm talking about.

Biological sex can't be changed afterward (well, in principle you could before birth with genetic manipulation...), so you can't choose it at will. Therefore, society tries to fill that gap through social systems so that fewer people suffer because of it.

For now I don't pay much attention to my appearance and no one says anything, but perhaps that era is coming to an end.

### For Now, I'm Glad I'm a Man
This might sound very discriminatory to say, but to be blunt, I often feel that men are still in a dominant position in the world today. It varies by country, but men clearly seem to have more freedom.

I believe these disparities should be eliminated. But the situation still differs greatly by country, and even if societal rules change, people's common sense doesn't change immediately. Rather, such ways of thinking have come to support parts of the current social system, and it can be difficult to dismantle them.

So far, being a man hasn't prevented me from doing what I want or caused people to look at me with disdain. So frankly, I'm glad I'm a man. But that's precisely why trying to become cute might reveal something.

I wish someone would help me. It feels like going against society, and some people have a strong aversion to that. I don't have the courage.
