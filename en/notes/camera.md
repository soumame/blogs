Previously I used to say things like [[en/misc/wishlist-2025-12|I can't buy what I want]], but after starting a [[en/works/diver-x|part-time job]] and being allowed to do what I want, I've slowly been saving up money.
So I'm thinking of buying a camera.

What, not buying VR? ~~I can't say I won't buy one for now because there's a BigScreen at my workplace and nothing else feels like VR~~ I can't say that, but since I once sold mine before, maybe a camera first.

My budget is within 150,000 yen. By "camera" I don't mean something at the smartphone level — I want something that gives a nice bokeh, satisfies a little bit of ownership desire, and won't leave me unable to take shots because of performance limitations.

## Cameras I've Bought in the Past
First, before buying a new one, I'll look back at the past.
### IXY 130
In 2014, around the time I started developing strong interests in various things, my parents bought me this as a birthday or Christmas present (my birthday is December 15th, so presents often get "merged"). It cost 12,000 yen. For an eight-year-old boy, a 12,000 yen camera isn't cheap.

Simple operation, limited features, and unlike toy cameras for kids, it actually took decent photos and had an optical zoom.
It's a typical compact camera — image quality below today's smartphone cameras, but it's an important memory.
[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/322cacd716bb48055bdf9c2b666f952f.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/322cacd716bb48055bdf9c2b666f952f)
The first photo I took (that I could show others) was from a trip to Hokkaido during the spring break of third grade. It reminds me of the excitement of being on a plane. Back then my parents took me with JAL award tickets, so the experience of flying was comfortable and thrilling — a far cry from now, when a six-hour flight on airasia (a Southeast Asian international LCC) makes your butt go numb and it's painful.

Back then I didn't carry a smartphone-like device everywhere like I do now, and this was my only intentional way to record images and memories.


### Power shot SX720 HS
[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/de901908de04fc7c8a591a6644d08809.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/de901908de04fc7c8a591a6644d08809)
Later, in 2019. After five years of use and getting a bit wiser, I started feeling the camera's performance was insufficient.
Again it was a combined Christmas and birthday present from my parents. It cost 36,000 yen. I'm really impressed my parents bought a camera at that price for a middle schooler (I didn't understand how hard it was to earn money at the time).

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/25c60a06c0dd6eb80c73d5bbd01ba5b4.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/25c60a06c0dd6eb80c73d5bbd01ba5b4)
Maybe I discovered train photography as image quality improved. The E217 series feels like a sign of the times. That line doesn't run on those tracks anymore. Telephoto is great.

I remember once dropping this camera in water and sending it for repair.

But I didn't use it as much as the IXY130.
When I became a middle schooler my parents bought me a smartphone and it covered most needs. My first smartphone was an iPhone 7 (one of those practically free au phones), and I was amazed by its performance. Even a compact camera costing over 30,000 yen is, in hindsight, only around that entry-level price. Smartphone cameras, which were gaining momentum then, started to match them.
It had no real bokeh, and honestly its advantages were... but this camera had a palm-sized 40x optical zoom, which was extremely useful.

After that I started using phones like the POCO X3 and devices like the Google Pixel 6 with multiple cameras, so I stopped using dedicated cameras as much.

### Nikon D40
However, while using smartphones I began to feel that bokeh greatly changes the impression of a photo, and I wondered if there was any way to achieve that. Smartphone AI corrections and portrait modes detect subjects and artificially add blur, but it still feels very different from optical bokeh.

From a closet at home came a dust-covered SLR, a Nikon D40. It used to belong to my parents.

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/ed1c75a14b9cdc2befc6441878f848b2.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/ed1c75a14b9cdc2befc6441878f848b2)
This is from when I suddenly dismantled a DVD drive for some reason.
The bokeh really is nice.

It was in pretty good condition and all functions worked perfectly. I used it a fair amount to shoot small objects. Due to the sensor characteristics, colors had a film-like, somewhat "emo" look (lack of vocabulary). Although it has AF, it was effectively manual, and low-light performance was poor — noise was intense. I didn't take it out of the house that much. Tweaking settings, bracing my hands to avoid blur, watching light levels, and pressing the shutter is kind of fun in its own way.

### Osmo Pocket 3
I might have started getting conscious about "cameras" only from 2024.
I had been satisfied with smartphone cameras, but during [[en/accomplishments/mitoujr-2024|未踏ジュニア]] I had to film things I made and record myself speaking to share them with the world (on the Internet), and I started feeling some limits to smartphone cameras. After about 30 minutes of filming, a phone would stop recording.

So I bought an Osmo Pocket 3 with the Mitou Junior budget. At the time we were live streaming announcement events, and this camera was useful for that. It's still actively used and I carry it everywhere, so the body has a crack 😱

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/36b6beb118f4e4e789925624533f4569.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/36b6beb118f4e4e789925624533f4569)

This camera is really easy to use and small, so it's still often in my bag. The UX is just too good. It doesn't offer many fine-grained settings, but it lets you quickly shoot video. For stills, in some cases it might even outperform a smartphone camera. Of course, it has no wide-angle or telephoto (there are attachments for wide-angle), and it lacks the insanely powerful AI corrections that make nonexistent objects appear like some recent Pixels.

### Pixel 9 Pro
It's become an era where a smartphone is often enough to take the photos I want — maybe starting with the Pixel 9.
It was the first phone I bought with my own money after graduating high school.

The Pixel 9 Pro is really high-level, and I love the HDR function. Brightness information of the photos is displayed even in the phone's preview.

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/51c630f0ddbe500ff46fe017e51b960c.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/51c630f0ddbe500ff46fe017e51b960c)
This one is from when I recently went to Odawara. If you use a device that supports HDR previews, parts of the image should appear brighter.

Honestly, for close distances this is fine, and I'm pretty satisfied. It's great to be able to quickly take a shot when you want to. There aren't many settings and it's all auto, but in an era where people take lots of photos and upload many of them without editing, this can be better.

## I Want to Take Better Photos
My current gear is good enough, but sometimes I want to take even better still photos. When I borrow a camera from someone around me and shoot, the difference from a smartphone camera is clear.

I've been wavering about this for about a year, and I had roughly 150,000 yen saved up to buy a camera.

People often tell me I spend money recklessly or that I'm extravagant. My family doesn't usually spend tens of thousands on hobbies; maybe we don't have many long-term hobbies or entertainments. Or maybe I just haven't seen it much. Anyway, since there weren't people like that at home, I hesitated to buy. There are other reasons too.

It's not something you need to live, and it might not make money, but I still think it can enrich life.

So I'm thinking of buying a camera. In short, it's desire for something. I also think cameras have a big pay-to-win element, so I want an interchangeable-lens camera.

### Choosing My First Camera
My camera knowledge is shallow, so I thought an entry-level model would be good.
But I've handled higher-end gear already — a friend's SONY α7IV, the BlackMagic we used when [[en/works/dmcouncil|events were short-staffed]], the ROS R5 (I think?), and an EOS R6 Mark II I borrowed at [[en/works/keio|university]]. In other words, I've touched expensive models first. So when I handle cheap entry-level models in stores, I strongly notice the difference.

That made choosing my own camera hard. Living with my parents allows me to save most of my part-time pay, but given my savings, including the kit lens, 150,000 yen is about my limit.

Looking around, the candidates were:
- Nikon Z50
- EOS R50
- Nikon Zfc
- Sony a6400
There are others, but in terms of reputation, balance, and cost performance, it seemed to be these.

Among them, the EOS R50 felt like it had outstanding cost performance. I tried it in stores and the AF speed was close to higher-end models, and it was newer. Personally I preferred Nikon's mechanical feel, but for the convenience of being able to toss it in a bag and carry it everywhere, I ended up leaning toward the EOS R50. That would mean a sequence of purchases across brands like IXY, PowerShot, then EOS. I was curious about other manufacturers, but I might settle on Canon after all.

## Going to Buy the Camera
At first I thought I could just buy it on Amazon, but I'm still 19 and my card limit won't go above 100,000 yen so I couldn't pay, and I saw a sale at an electronics retailer, so I decided to buy there. Also, cheap Amazon listings are usually from marketplace sellers and the warranty situation seemed sketchy, so for purchases like this an electronics retailer felt stronger.

I chose a kit with two lenses (a zoom lens and a telephoto lens).

The price at the electronics store was about 125,000 yen. That felt cheap for two lenses. With cashback it was even a bit cheaper. I skipped the Amazon marketplace and went to the electronics store I hadn't visited in a while.

I spent a few hours deciding whether to buy points and peripheral camera accessories.
