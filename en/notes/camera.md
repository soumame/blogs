A while ago I was saying things like [[en/misc/wishlist-2025-12|I can't buy what I want]], but I started [[en/works/diver-x|a part-time job]] and have been able to save up some money while getting to do what I want. So, I'm thinking about buying a camera.

What, not buying VR? ~~I can't say "there's a BigScreen at my workplace so nothing else feels like VR and I won't buy one for the time being"~~ I can't say that, but since I once sold one before, I'll probably get a camera first.

My budget is within ¥150,000. By "camera" I don't mean a smartphone level thing — I want something that gives nice bokeh, satisfies the desire to own something a bit, and won't prevent me from capturing what I want because of its performance.

## Cameras I've Bought in the Past
First, before buying a new one, let's look back at the past.

### IXY 130
In 2014, when I was beginning to develop strong interests in various things, my parents bought this for me as a birthday or Christmas present (my birthday is December 15, so presents often "merge"). ¥12,000. For an eight-year-old, a ¥12,000 camera isn't cheap.

Simple operation, limited features, and yet unlike toy kids' cameras it actually took decent photos and had optical zoom. It was a typical compact camera; image quality was below today's smartphone cameras, but it holds important memories.
[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/322cacd716bb48055bdf9c2b666f952f.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/322cacd716bb48055bdf9c2b666f952f)
The first photo I took that I could show people was from a trip to Hokkaido during spring break in third grade. It reminds me of the excitement of being on a plane. Back then my parents took us with JAL award tickets, so flying was comfortable and thrilling — a far cry from now, when a six-hour flight on AirAsia (a Southeast Asian low-cost carrier) leaves you feeling numb in the butt.

Back then I didn't carry a smartphone all the time like I do now, and this was the only way I had to intentionally record images.


### PowerShot SX720 HS
[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/de901908de04fc7c8a591a6644d08809.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/de901908de04fc7c8a591a6644d08809)
Then, in 2019. After using the IXY for five years I started to feel its performance wasn't enough. Again, parents bought this as a combined birthday/Christmas gift. ¥36,000. It's amazing my parents bought a camera at that price for a middle schooler (I didn't really understand how hard it is to earn money back then).

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/25c60a06c0dd6eb80c73d5bbd01ba5b4.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/25c60a06c0dd6eb80c73d5bbd01ba5b4)
Maybe I discovered train photography because the image quality improved. The E217 series gives you a sense of the era. That track doesn't run anymore. Telephoto is great.

I remember once dropping this camera in water and having to send it in for repair.

But I didn't use it as much as the IXY130. When I became a middle-schooler my parents gave me a smartphone and it covered most needs. My first smartphone was an iPhone 7 (some kind of nearly free phone on au), and its performance amazed me. Even though the compact camera cost over ¥30,000, in hindsight it was roughly an entry-level compact. Smartphone cameras, which were growing rapidly then, were becoming comparable. There was no bokeh, and to be honest, what it beat the phone at...

That said, this camera had an amazing feature: a palm-sized body with 40x optical zoom, which I used a lot.

Afterwards I started using phones like the POCO X3 and phones with multiple cameras like the Google Pixel 6, so I didn't use dedicated cameras as much.


### Nikon D40
Using smartphones made me realize how much bokeh can change the impression of a photo, and I wondered if there was any way to achieve that. Smartphone AI corrections and portrait modes detect subjects and artificially add blur, but optical bokeh feels significantly different.

From the closet at home I found a dusty DSLR, a Nikon D40. It used to belong to my parents.

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/ed1c75a14b9cdc2befc6441878f848b2.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/ed1c75a14b9cdc2befc6441878f848b2)
This was taken when I suddenly disassembled a DVD drive for some reason.
Bokeh really is nice.

Its condition was pretty good and all functions worked perfectly. I used it a fair amount, taking photos of small objects. Due to the sensor characteristics the colors sometimes looked film-like, giving what you'd call an "emo" photo (I lack the vocabulary). AF exists but it's basically manual, and it's weak in low light — the noise is intense. I didn't take it out and shoot all that much. Tweaking settings, keeping your hands steady, watching the light, and pressing the shutter can be fun though.


### Osmo Pocket 3
Maybe it was 2024 that I really started thinking about "cameras." I was satisfied with smartphone cameras, but while working on [[en/accomplishments/mitoujr-2024|Mitou Junior]] I had to film what I made and my talks to share them on the Internet, and I started to feel some limits with smartphones. After about 30 minutes of recording my phone would stop recording.

So I used the budget from Mitou Junior to buy an Osmo Pocket 3. At the time I was live-streaming presentations, and this camera came in handy. It still gets a lot of use and I carry it everywhere, to the point that the body has a crack 😱

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/36b6beb118f4e4e789925624533f4569.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/36b6beb118f4e4e789925624533f4569)

This camera is truly easy to use and small, so it's still often in my bag. The UX is great. You can't tweak many settings, but it records video quickly. For stills it can even beat a smartphone in some cases. Of course it has no wide-angle or telephoto (there are attachments for wide), and it doesn't have the kind of powerful AI correction that makes nonexistent details visible like recent Pixels.


### Pixel 9 Pro
I felt that smartphones had reached the point where they could handle what I wanted to shoot with the Pixel 9. This was the first phone I bought with my own money after graduating high school.

The Pixel 9 Pro is extremely capable; I especially like the HDR feature. The brightness information of a photo is shown even in the phone's preview.

[![Image from Gyazo](https://i.gyazo.com/51c630f0ddbe500ff46fe017e51b960c.jpg)](https://gyazo.com/51c630f0ddbe500ff46fe017e51b960c)
This is from a recent trip to Odawara. If you use a device that supports HDR preview, part of the image should appear brighter.

Honestly, for close distances this is good enough and I'm pretty satisfied. It's great that you can snap a photo whenever you want. There aren't many settings to tweak — it's mostly automatic — but in an era where you take lots of photos and upload many of them without editing, that's sometimes preferable.

## I Want to Take Better Photos
My current gear is good enough, but sometimes I want to take even better still photos. When people around me bring cameras and let me try them, the difference from a smartphone is obvious.

I've been agonizing over this for about a year, and I had set aside around ¥150,000 to buy a camera.

My parents often tell me I spend money recklessly or lavishly, and my family isn't the type to spend tens of thousands on hobbies. Maybe it's a household with relatively few ongoing hobbies or entertainments. Or maybe I just haven't seen it much. Either way, there wasn't anyone at home who spent like that, so I hesitated to buy. There are other reasons too.

It's not something you need to live, and even if it's considered non-profitable, I still think it can enrich your life.

So, I'm leaning toward buying a camera. In short, it's desire. And since cameras have a big "pay to win" element, I want an interchangeable-lens camera.


### Choosing My First Camera
My knowledge of cameras is shallow, so I thought an entry-level model might be best. But I've already had chances to handle higher-end gear: a friend's Sony α7IV, a BlackMagic used when events were understaffed ([[en/works/dmcouncil|when events were understaffed]]), maybe a RED R5, and an EOS R6 Mark II borrowed from [[en/works/keio|university]]. So I had already touched expensive cameras. That means when I try cheap entry-level bodies in stores, I feel the difference strongly.

That made choosing my own camera difficult. Since I still live at my parents' house I can save a decent part of my wages, but considering the money I have, including kit lenses, about ¥150,000 is my limit.

Looking around, the options were:
- Nikon Z50
- EOS R50
- Nikon Zfc
- Sony a6400
There are others, but in terms of reputation, balance, and cost performance, these seemed to be the ones.

Among them, the EOS R50 felt like the best value. I tried one in a store; its AF speed is close to that of higher-end models, and it's also a newer release. Personally I prefer Nikon's mechanical feel, but for the convenience of tossing it in my bag and taking it anywhere, the EOS R50 felt right. That means my purchases went in the order of IXY, PowerShot, and then EOS. I was curious about other brands, but I may end up settling on Canon.


## Going to Buy the Camera
At first I thought I could just buy it on Amazon, but since I'm still 19 my card limit won't go above ¥100,000 so I couldn't pay that way, and I saw a sale at an electronics retailer, so I decided to buy there. Also, Amazon's cheap listings are often marketplace sellers with questionable warranties, so in cases like this a brick-and-mortar electronics store felt strong.

I went with a kit that included two lenses (a zoom lens and a telephoto).

The electronics store price was about ¥125,000. That felt cheap for two lenses. With cashback it was even a little cheaper. I skipped Amazon's marketplace and went to an electronics store I hadn't visited in a while.

It took several hours to decide whether to buy accessories and camera gear that would earn points.
